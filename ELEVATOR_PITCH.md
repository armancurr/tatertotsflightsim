# What is Flight Simulator?

> **The one-liner:** a **real flight simulator that runs in a web browser** — you open a page,
> and you're sitting in a Cessna 172 on a runway, with mountains ahead, clouds overhead, and a
> living world below. No download, no install, no plug-in. Just a URL.

Flight Simulator is a complete, playable 3D flight simulator built from scratch with
**Three.js (WebGL2) and TypeScript**. You start on runway 36 at a small airfield, push the
throttle up, rotate at 55 knots, and fly: climb, turn, stall and recover, cruise over farmland,
a river, a coastal city and snow-capped mountains, then come back and land — or crash, watch the
smoke rise, and press **R** to try again. Everything you see and hear is generated by code in
the browser: the terrain, the trees, the towns, the sky, the aircraft, even the engine sound.
There isn't a single downloaded image, model or sound file. It's built on **three pillars:**

## 1. It flies like a real Cessna
The aircraft is a **Cessna 172RG Cutlass** (the real retractable-gear 172). Its flight model is a
proper six-degrees-of-freedom physics simulation — lift that grows with speed and angle of attack
and collapses past the stall, drag that grows with flaps and gear, an engine and propeller that
lag behind the throttle, wheels on springs, brakes, nosewheel steering, ground effect near the
runway, P-factor that pulls the nose left at full power. It's tuned to the real pilot's handbook:
it stalls near 48 knots clean and 42 with full flaps, lifts off around 55, climbs about 700 feet
per minute, cruises around 125 knots, and glides about 9 to 1. Landings are rated from "butter"
to "hard"; bad ones end in a crash with a reason ("Hard landing — gear collapsed", "Gear-up
landing", "Prop strike", "Ditched in water", "Hit an obstacle").

## 2. A world that looks real
A large procedural world — you can fly for tens of kilometres in any direction and it never runs
out. A patchwork of farmland, forests with individual trees, a river winding to the sea, beaches
and islands, two lakes, a coastal city, a town and villages, roads, and a 2,500-metre mountain
range to the north. A physically based sky with a real sun, aerial haze that turns distant
mountains blue, soft shadows, drifting cumulus clouds, and four times of day from dawn to golden
hour. The airport has FAA runway markings, edge and approach lights and a working PAPI (the
red/white glide-slope lights). Four camera views — cockpit with a live instrument panel, chase,
fly-by and orbit — blend smoothly into each other, and a steam-gauge HUD shows every instrument a
172 pilot relies on.

## 3. Built like a professional product
- **Tested:** over a thousand automated tests — unit tests, "fuzz" tests that throw thousands of
  random inputs at the physics and controls, scripted whole flights (take-off → turns → stall →
  landing), and end-to-end tests that start the real game in a test browser and fly it with the
  keyboard.
- **Pure physics:** the flight model is a separate, deterministic module (`step(state, controls,
  dt)`), so every rule of flight can be proved by a test.
- **Tunable:** every number (mass, wing area, lift and drag, stall angle, engine power, camera
  distances, fog, lighting) lives in one config file — balancing is one edit.
- **Fast:** designed for 60 frames per second with instancing, level of detail, background
  workers and culling; it reports its own frame rate on `window.sim`.

## Where it stands (2026-09-22)
The full flight works end to end and holds 60 fps on an Apple M2 Max. A wave of realism work
(volumetric clouds, detailed buildings, moving cars, ships, other aircraft, better terrain,
water and trees, the full airport, audio and effects polish) was in progress and is merged at the
stop point; `RESUME.md` says exactly what is done and what is next.
